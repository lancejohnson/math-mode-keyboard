Angles and radians

An angle is an amount of turning. Radians measure that turning with the circle's own radius — a piece of string you wrap around the edge.

StartPredict first

Guess before you look. Being surprised is where the learning happens.

1Turn the angle

Drag the pink dot around. Turning counterclockwise is positive; keep going past a full turn, or go clockwise past 0 for negative angles.

    2Degrees to radians in your head

    One fact does all the work: a half turn is 180° = π radians. Step through, then try another.

    3Use the model to solve

    A bike wheel has radius 30 cm. Turn the wheel until the valve (pink dot) has turned 2.5 radians. How far has the bike rolled?

      Now without the model: how far does the bike roll in one full turn? (A number in cm, or use π.)

      4Draw it yourself

      Draw the end side of this angle: a straight line from the centre out toward where the turn finishes. Start at 3 o'clock and think about which way to turn.

        5Practice

        Eight mixed questions. Type answers like 3π/4, 3pi/4, 135 or 2.36.

        6Explain it

        A full turn is 2π radians on a coin and on the Earth's equator. Why doesn't the size of the circle matter?

        A strong answer says:

        • Radians count the arc in radius-lengths, so the circle's own size is the unit.
        • The distance around any circle is 2π times its radius, so a full turn is always 2π radius-lengths: 2π radians.
        • Scale the circle up and the arc and the radius grow together; their ratio stays the same.
